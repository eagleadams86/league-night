Privacy Policy

Effective 7 September 2026

A league stays in this browser until you sign in and share it. There is no account required to use the app, and nothing is uploaded until you choose to.

What Is Stored

Each league you create, join or restore — its players or teams, seasons, fixtures, scores and tournaments — saved in this browser's local storage on this device, one entry per league, beside a record of which leagues this device holds, your choice of colour theme and which tab you last had open. Clearing your browser's site data removes them; Forget This League on the League tab removes one.

Names are the only free text a league holds: player, team, season and league names, and a venue, each capped at forty characters. There is no notes field.

If you share or join a league, its Admin Key is kept in this browser's local storage too, under a key of its own, so the app can show it to you and use it. It is never put into the league itself, so it reaches no backup, no share link and nothing that is uploaded. One more flag records whether this browser has a live sign-in, so a signed-in visit is recognised without pressing anything — and so a browser that has never signed in fetches nothing from Google at all.

What Is Not

  • No analytics, no tracking, no cookies.
  • No advertising, and nothing sold or shared with anyone.
  • Until you sign in, no third-party script of any kind: the page loads nothing beyond its own files. Signing in is the one exception, and it is described below — Google's sign-in client and the Firebase library are fetched from Google only once you ask to share or join a league, or if you have signed in on this browser before.
  • No personal information is asked for. Signing in tells the app the name and email address on your Google account, and nothing else.

Backups and Share Links

A backup is a plain JSON file of one league, written to wherever your browser puts downloads. It never leaves your device unless you send it somewhere yourself.

A read-only share link carries a snapshot of the league inside the link itself — nothing is uploaded to make one — and never carries the League ID, so a link is a picture of the league and not a way in. Whoever holds it can read everything inside it.

Sharing a League

Sharing a league across devices uses an optional Google sign-in and a Firebase project the author pays for. Nothing is sent until you sign in, and Google's code is not even fetched until you reach for one of the buttons that signs in, shares a league or joins one, or have signed in on this browser before. Once you share a league, the league itself — its names, fixtures and scores — is stored in that project, readable by anyone signed in to Google who knows its League ID, and writable by its admins. Your account's display name, the list of leagues you have joined and which of them you were last in are stored beside it so the app can show who is a member, find your leagues on a new phone and open on the right one. That record holds ids and names, nothing else. The Admin Key is stored in a document nobody can read.

An invite link carries the League ID — and the Admin Key, if the sender chose to include it — in the part of the address after the #, which never reaches a server; the app takes it off the address bar the moment the link is opened. The QR code is drawn by the app itself; nothing is fetched to make one.

Signing out stops the app talking to Google. Leaving a league removes your name from it; the owner deleting a league removes it for everyone. The security rules are published in the repository.

Changes

If what the app stores ever changes, this page and its effective date are updated in the same commit as the change.
